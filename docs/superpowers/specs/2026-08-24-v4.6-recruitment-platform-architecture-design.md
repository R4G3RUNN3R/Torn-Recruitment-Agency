# v4.6+ Recruitment Platform Architecture Design

**Date:** 2026-08-24  
**Status:** Approved architecture; written-spec review pending  
**Repository:** `R4G3RUNN3R/Torn-Recruitment-Agency`

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the long-term architecture for expanding Recruitment Agency beyond v4.5.4 into a structured Torn recruitment platform for both companies and factions.

The central rule is strict separation of recruitment intent while preserving one authoritative shared intelligence identity per Torn player.

A player may therefore have:

- one shared Player Intelligence record;
- zero or one Company Recruitment record;
- zero or one Faction Recruitment record.

Company and Faction recruitment state must never be merged merely because both refer to the same Torn player ID.

## 2. Non-negotiable invariants

1. **One Torn ID = one authoritative shared Player Intelligence identity.**
2. **Company Recruitment and Faction Recruitment are separate domains.**
3. Shared Scout/intelligence refreshes update the player once and then trigger independent Company/Faction recalculation.
4. Company pipeline state, notes, follow-ups, campaigns, salary, outcomes, tags and messaging state never mutate Faction recruitment state.
5. Faction pipeline state, waivers, specialist profiles, follow-ups, campaigns, outcomes, tags and messaging state never mutate Company recruitment state.
6. A player discovered only for Company Recruitment must not gain a phantom Faction Recruitment record, and vice versa.
7. Recruitment-facing functions are separate routed tabs rather than combined mega-pages.
8. Shared intelligence may be displayed in both recruitment domains, but contextual conclusions remain domain-specific.
9. Opening a Torn profile, forum source or message compose window does not automatically change recruitment stage.
10. Torn messages remain manual-send only. No automatic or bulk message submission.
11. All Torn API work continues through the shared scheduler with a hard ceiling of 75 requests/minute and at least 800 ms between Recruitment Agency Torn API calls.
12. Database migrations remain additive. Existing user data must not be destructively reset to introduce this architecture.
13. The strict Global Intelligence privacy whitelist remains unchanged unless a future, explicitly reviewed design requires an addition.

## 3. Domain model

### 3.1 Shared Player Intelligence

The shared record contains facts and observations about the Torn player independent of recruitment purpose, including where available:

- Torn player ID;
- current/observed name and name history;
- level;
- Scout latest values and history;
- activity observations;
- Xanax/refill/attack/RW history used by Scout;
- EE;
- current-company observations/history;
- net worth where already supported;
- Fit and Fit history;
- last-active data;
- intelligence freshness metadata;
- Global Intelligence observations permitted by the existing whitelist;
- shared intelligence-change history.

A manual Scout lookup may create a shared Player Intelligence record even when the player is not a Company or Faction prospect.

Shared intelligence remains stored until deliberate cleanup/reset rather than being automatically deleted merely because there is no current recruitment record.

### 3.2 Company Recruitment record

Created only when the player is relevant to Company Recruitment.

It may contain:

- Company pipeline state and recruitment cycles;
- Company discovery provenance/forum observations;
- Company recruiter notes and pinned note;
- Company tags;
- Company follow-ups;
- Company campaigns;
- Company baseline eligibility;
- vacancy Match results;
- pinned/suggested vacancy;
- Company Opportunity score;
- contact history/outcomes;
- Do Not Contact state;
- salary/offer history;
- Company waivers;
- archive state;
- Company-specific timeline events.

### 3.3 Faction Recruitment record

Created only when the player is relevant to Faction Recruitment.

It may contain:

- Faction pipeline state and recruitment cycles;
- Faction discovery provenance/forum observations;
- Faction recruiter notes and pinned note;
- Faction tags;
- Faction follow-ups;
- Faction campaigns;
- Faction Baseline result;
- specialist-profile results;
- pinned/suggested primary specialist profile;
- Faction Opportunity score;
- waiver records;
- contact history/outcomes;
- Do Not Contact state;
- archive state;
- Faction-specific timeline events.

The existing managed application shell remains the navigation host. Settings remains accessible through the top-right Settings control only and must not reappear as a duplicate sidebar entry.

Sidebar groups are independently collapsible and more than one may remain expanded. Expansion state is persisted locally. The application remembers the last open routed tab.

Users may later hide unused tabs and reorder tabs within their own section while preserving a sane default order. Simple/Advanced mode remains supported; advanced functions can be hidden in Simple mode without deleting state.

## 5. Company Recruitment design

### 5.1 Pipeline

The approved canonical Company pipeline for the future architecture is:

1. Not Contacted
2. Contacted
3. Replied
4. Shortlisted
5. Hired
6. Rejected

The current v4.5 runtime/test suite is known to contain a separate ordering discrepancy in which `Shortlisted` appears before `Contacted`. This architecture does **not** authorize silently changing that runtime order during foundation work or an unrelated change. Reordering the live/runtime pipeline to the approved canonical order requires its own explicit implementation decision, migration review and regression updates.

### 5.2 Company baseline

Company may define company-wide recruitment requirements. Each criterion is independently classified as:

- **Hard:** required for normal eligibility;
- **Preferred:** contributes to suitability but does not block eligibility.

A failed Company baseline Hard requirement marks the candidate `NOT CURRENTLY ELIGIBLE` for normal hire, but does not block discovery, contact, reply handling, evaluation, follow-ups or campaigns. `Hired` is blocked until all Company baseline Hard failures are either genuinely satisfied or individually waived.

### 5.3 Vacancies

Vacancies are the Company-specific recruitment profiles. They replace any need for a parallel Company specialist-profile concept.

A vacancy contains, at minimum:

- name/role;
- number of openings;
- status;
- Hard and Preferred requirements;
- scoring weights;
- salary budget/expectation fields where relevant;
- availability expectations;
- notes;
- version metadata.

Vacancy states are:

- Draft
- Open
- Paused
- Filled
- Archived

Multiple openings for the same role are represented by one vacancy with an openings count rather than duplicated vacancy records.

### 5.4 Vacancy evaluation

Every relevant Company candidate is evaluated locally against every active vacancy using known intelligence.

The system:

- surfaces the best eligible vacancy as an automatic suggestion;
- displays other meaningful Match results;
- allows the recruiter to manually pin a different vacancy;
- never silently replaces a manually pinned vacancy;
- may notify that the calculated best suggestion has changed.

A failed vacancy-specific Hard requirement makes the candidate `NOT ELIGIBLE` for that vacancy while preserving and displaying the raw Match score. This mirrors Faction specialist behaviour and preserves useful information about developing or near-matching candidates.

Editing a vacancy or requirement profile recalculates affected Company Match values locally and does not require a Torn API call.

## 6. Faction Recruitment design

### 6.1 Faction pipeline

Faction Recruitment uses its own pipeline:

1. Prospect
2. Contacted
3. Replied
4. Evaluating
5. Invite Ready
6. Joined
7. Rejected
8. Deferred

### 6.2 Faction Baseline

Faction Baseline defines faction-wide standards. Every criterion is independently Hard or Preferred.

A failed Hard baseline requirement produces:

- `NOT CURRENTLY ELIGIBLE`;
- continued ability to remain Prospect, Contacted, Replied, Evaluating or Deferred;
- blocking of `Invite Ready` unless all failed Hard baseline requirements are either genuinely satisfied or individually waived.

Passing the Faction Baseline is sufficient for `Invite Ready`. A candidate does not need to qualify for any specialist profile.

### 6.3 Specialist profiles

Faction may create multiple named specialist profiles, for example:

- RW Fighter;
- Chain Specialist;
- Crime Specialist;
- Developing Member;
- High Activity Member;
- offensive/defensive specialisations.

There is no arbitrary hard maximum. Only active profiles are evaluated/displayed prominently.

Each specialist profile may contain Hard and Preferred criteria.

A Hard failure inside a specialist profile means the candidate is `NOT ELIGIBLE` for that specialist profile, but the raw Match score is still calculated and shown.

All active specialist profiles are evaluated. The best eligible specialist profile is suggested automatically. The recruiter may manually pin a different primary specialist profile. A manual pin survives later rescoring; the system may flag that the calculated best suggestion has changed but must not overwrite the pin.

## 7. Hard-requirement waivers

Company and Faction Hard requirements may be explicitly waived per individual requirement.

Waivers are never blanket overrides.

Each waiver records:

- recruitment domain;
- requirement/profile context;
- exact waived Hard requirement;
- reason;
- granted timestamp;
- optional review date;
- current state;
- resolved timestamp/reason where applicable.

A waiver changes eligibility semantics to `Eligible by Waiver`; it must not falsely convert the underlying failed requirement into `PASS`.

If fresh intelligence later shows the player genuinely satisfies the waived requirement, the waiver automatically becomes `Resolved` while remaining in immutable history.

If an optional waiver review date is reached, the waiver becomes `Review Due` but remains active until the recruiter acts. It must not silently expire and unexpectedly block eligibility.

## 8. Recruitment operations

### 8.1 Overview vs Today

Overview and Today remain separate tabs in each recruitment domain.

- **Overview** provides strategic status, KPIs and concise summaries linking to dedicated analytical tabs.
- **Today** is an action queue containing work that deserves attention now.

Today may surface:

- replies;
- overdue follow-ups;
- stale candidates;
- newly discovered candidates;
- newly eligible candidates;
- domain-specific consequences of shared intelligence changes;
- opportunity windows;
- candidates requiring re-evaluation.

Company Today and Faction Today do not merge their queues.

### 8.2 Follow-ups

Company and Faction follow-ups are separate.

A follow-up supports:

- date;
- optional time;
- reason;
- optional note;
- optional recurrence.

Overdue follow-ups remain overdue until completed or rescheduled. They are not automatically rolled forward or expired.

### 8.3 Stage Aging

Stage-aging thresholds are configurable per stage and separately for Company/Faction.

Stale candidates:

- receive a visible warning;
- surface in the corresponding Today/action queue;
- are never automatically moved to another stage.

### 8.4 Contact outcomes

Contact outcome is separate from pipeline stage.

Built-in outcomes are provided and custom outcomes are supported. This allows combinations such as `Stage: Replied` with `Outcome: Salary Too High` without abusing pipeline state.

### 8.5 Do Not Contact

Do Not Contact is a dedicated explicit flag, not a pipeline stage and not merely a tag.

When active:

- the candidate remains visible;
- normal Message actions are blocked/suppressed;
- explicit override is required to proceed.

### 8.6 Recruitment Sessions

Company and Faction each have their own Recruitment Sessions tab.

Sessions may be seeded from:

- a saved view/filter; or
- the relevant domain's intelligent priority queue.

The interface presents one candidate at a time and advances to the next after an explicit recruiter action. Session completion provides a concise outcome summary.

### 8.7 Bulk operations

Allowed bulk operations include:

- Scout;
- stage changes;
- tags;
- campaign assignment;
- follow-ups;
- archive;
- export.

Automatic bulk message sending is not permitted.

## 9. Recruitment campaigns and saved organisation

Company campaigns and Faction campaigns are separate user-facing systems.

A campaign represents a real recruitment objective and may contain:

- title;
- target;
- start/end dates;
- vacancy/profile targeting;
- candidate membership;
- progress;
- outcomes;
- metrics.

A candidate may belong to multiple campaigns.

Saved Views and tag libraries are also separate between Company and Faction.

Company additionally gets a Talent Pool for strong prospects not in active recruitment. Faction uses the explicit `Deferred` stage rather than duplicating this concept with a second talent-pool abstraction.

## 10. Recruitment cycles and historical truth

A single recruitment record may contain multiple recruitment cycles.

Example:

- January: Contacted -> Rejected;
- August: Reactivated -> Replied -> Hired.

The system must not create duplicate candidate identities merely because a player re-enters recruitment.

Historical `Hired` or `Joined` events remain true even if the player later leaves. Departure is represented as a subsequent event rather than rewriting the earlier outcome.

Archived candidates are removed from active operational views but retained in history and analytics. Deliberate deletion requires confirmation. Deleting a Company record does not delete a Faction record and vice versa. Shared intelligence survives unless separately cleaned up.

## 11. Timelines, provenance and notes

### 11.1 Timeline

Each recruitment domain has its own timeline. It may include:

- recruitment actions;
- stage changes;
- follow-ups;
- contact outcomes;
- campaign changes;
- domain Match/eligibility changes;
- waiver events;
- relevant shared-intelligence changes as contextual references;
- recruiter notes.

System-generated events are immutable. Recruiter-authored notes may be edited or deleted.

### 11.2 Discovery provenance

Every recruitment record permanently retains:

- original discovery source;
- subsequent source observations;
- forum observations/history where applicable.

Repeated discovery of the same player merges into the same recruitment record rather than replacing provenance or creating duplicates.

Manual parser corrections preserve the original observed data and the corrected value.

### 11.3 Notes

Each recruitment domain supports:

- multiple timestamped notes;
- one optional pinned note.

Architecture should not prevent future custom fields, but arbitrary custom fields are not required in the first implementation phase.

## 12. Intelligence design

### 12.1 Shared refresh behaviour

A Scout refresh from any context updates the shared Player Intelligence record. Company and Faction consumers then recalculate independently from the same fresh facts.

Recruitment-specific state is not overwritten by the refresh.

### 12.2 Fit, Match and Opportunity

These are distinct concepts:

- **Fit:** general Scout-derived player/activity/value signal;
- **Match:** suitability for a Company vacancy or Faction specialist profile;
- **Opportunity:** domain-specific measure of how worthwhile it is to contact that player now.

Opportunity may use context such as:

- Match;
- Fit;
- availability;
- current employment;
- last-active state;
- intelligence freshness;
- contact history;
- pipeline stage;
- follow-up state;
- recent declines;
- meaningful intelligence changes.

Opportunity uses sensible defaults with configurable weights and must provide a fully explainable breakdown rather than an opaque number.

Opportunity is **not** a shared cross-domain recruitment page. Company Opportunity results live in Company Candidates/Today and the Company Opportunity Queue. Faction Opportunity results live in Faction Candidates/Today and the Faction Opportunity Queue.

### 12.3 Intelligence freshness and confidence

Major shared intelligence observations expose age/freshness, for example Fresh, Aging, Stale or Very Stale.

Stale values may still contribute to Match, but must reduce confidence and surface a warning rather than being silently treated as equally trustworthy.

Match therefore exposes a separate confidence indicator based on known-data coverage and freshness.

### 12.4 Smart Re-Scout

Smart Re-Scout is a dedicated shared-intelligence tab that identifies Player Intelligence records whose facts deserve refreshing, based on factors such as:

- stale shared data;
- missing important shared intelligence;
- explicit recruiter selection;
- recruitment importance supplied as prioritisation metadata without merging Company/Faction state.

It must not become an invisible background scanner. API calls remain explicit and pass through the shared scheduler.

### 12.5 Change Detection

Change Detection is a dedicated **shared Player Intelligence** tab. It contains changes to shared facts only, for example:

- current-company changes;
- newly companyless status;
- activity movement;
- Fit movement;
- name changes;
- last-active changes;
- freshness transitions.

It must not become a combined Company/Faction recruitment feed. Derived recruitment consequences are surfaced separately:

- Company eligibility, best-vacancy, Opportunity and Company-waiver changes -> Company Today/Timeline/Opportunity Queue;
- Faction eligibility, specialist, Opportunity and Faction-waiver changes -> Faction Today/Timeline/Opportunity Queue.

### 12.6 Candidate Compare

Company Compare and Faction Compare remain separate tabs. A comparison supports 2-5 candidates. Shared intelligence may be displayed in both contexts, but scores/recommendations are contextual to the relevant recruitment domain.

## 13. Company salary and workforce

### 13.1 Salary/offer history

Company recruitment supports structured salary/offer history, including:

- expected salary;
- proposed salary;
- counteroffer;
- agreed salary;
- negotiation state;
- timestamps.

This remains local/private recruitment data.

### 13.2 Workforce

The Workforce area extends Company recruitment after hire without erasing recruitment history.

Separate tabs cover:

- Staffing;
- Gap Analysis;
- Replacement Bench;
- Hired History.

Replacement Bench automatically ranks existing candidates against company roles/vacancies and also supports manual pinning of preferred backups. Automatic suggestions never silently overwrite recruiter pins.

## 14. Analytics

Company and Faction analytics remain independent.

Analytics support useful date presets plus custom date ranges.

Archived records remain part of historical analytics. Deliberately deleted records do not.

Source Performance distinguishes actual discovery origins rather than collapsing them into Company/Faction only.

Match Calibration may recommend scoring/profile changes based on historical outcomes, but must never automatically alter requirement weights or criteria.

## 15. Messaging

Company and Faction use separate template libraries.

Templates support:

- names;
- categories;
- approved placeholders.

The existing manual-send safety contract remains:

1. prepare locally;
2. optionally copy;
3. optionally open Torn compose addressed to the player;
4. user sends manually.

Message history distinguishes prepared, copied, compose-opened and confirmed-contact/outcome events. Opening compose does not imply `Contacted`.

## 16. Search, navigation productivity and undo

A universal search may find players by Torn ID/name and show where they exist:

- Player Intelligence;
- Company Recruitment;
- Faction Recruitment.

Advanced Mode may later expose a command palette and keyboard shortcuts.

Common local actions such as stage/tag/follow-up/archive changes should support a bounded Undo mechanism. The architecture does not require universal historical rollback of every operation.

## 17. Settings and notifications

Settings remains a single top-right routed page. It contains separate Company and Faction configuration sections rather than reintroducing sidebar Settings entries.

Internal sidebar badges may show meaningful actionable counts for:

- Company/Faction Today;
- Company/Faction Follow-ups;
- shared Change Detection;
- domain-specific stale/review items.

Optional browser notifications may be added later but must be off by default.

Overview pages may eventually permit rearranging/showing/hiding predefined widgets, but the architecture does not require a fully custom dashboard builder.

## 18. Export, backup and data safety

Exports are available independently for:

- Company recruitment;
- Faction recruitment;
- Shared Player Intelligence.

A full Recruitment Agency backup/restore format should support version validation and migration checks.

Database evolution remains additive. Existing DB12 stores and user data must not be destroyed merely to introduce the new domain model. Existing Smart Match and recruitment data should be mapped/migrated into the new architecture where applicable.

`NUKE IT ALL!` remains the explicit destructive reset for Recruitment Agency-owned browser-local data and must not affect unrelated browser/userscript data.

## 19. Global Intelligence privacy boundary

The existing strict Global Intelligence whitelist remains the network-sharing boundary unless separately reviewed in a later spec.

Recruitment-specific information remains local, including:

- Company/Faction pipeline state;
- notes;
- tags;
- salary/offer negotiation;
- follow-ups;
- campaigns;
- waivers;
- contact outcomes/history;
- vacancy/profile configuration;
- Company Match;
- Faction specialist Match;
- eligibility results;
- Opportunity scores;
- prepared messages;
- recruitment cycles.

Shared Global observations may be consumed locally by Company/Faction scoring without sending the resulting recruitment conclusions back to Global Intelligence.

## 20. Requirement/profile versioning

Company vacancies, Company baseline configuration, Faction Baseline and Faction specialist profiles are versioned sufficiently to explain historical evaluations.

Profiles/vacancies may be duplicated as templates for new ones.

Editing criteria/weights recalculates affected candidates locally using existing intelligence. Requirement edits alone must not consume Torn API calls.

Historical decisions retain enough version context to explain which requirements produced an earlier Match/eligibility result.

## 21. API scheduling and priority

All Recruitment Agency Torn API calls continue through one scheduler.

Hard constraints remain:

- maximum 75 Torn API calls/minute;
- minimum 800 ms spacing between Recruitment Agency Torn API calls.

Priority order:

1. explicit user-triggered Scout;
2. active recruitment operation;
3. passive enrichment.

No subsystem receives a private rate-limit bypass.

## 22. Delivery decomposition

This architecture is intentionally too large for one implementation PR. It defines the destination and boundaries; implementation must be split into focused follow-on specs/plans.

Recommended delivery order:

1. **Foundation** - shared Player Intelligence identity, separate Company/Faction recruitment domains, additive migration foundation, routed/collapsible navigation support.
2. **Company Recruitment** - Company-specific Overview/Today/Discover/Candidates/Pipeline, baseline/vacancies, campaigns and core operational tabs.
3. **Faction Recruitment** - independent pipeline, Faction Baseline, specialist profiles, eligibility, waivers and faction operational tabs.
4. **Intelligence Extensions** - freshness, confidence, domain Opportunity queues, Smart Re-Scout, shared Change Detection and contextual Compare.
5. **Workforce** - Staffing, Gap Analysis, Replacement Bench and Hired History.
6. **Analytics** - separate Company/Faction analytical tabs and calibration recommendations.
7. **Productivity** - Recruitment Sessions, richer navigation controls, command palette/keyboard support and dashboard refinements.

Each delivery slice must preserve the invariants in this document and be independently testable.

## 23. Acceptance principles for future implementation specs

Every follow-on implementation spec must explicitly verify, where relevant:

- one authoritative Player Intelligence identity per Torn ID;
- no Company/Faction recruitment-state bleed;
- recruitment records created only in the relevant domain;
- shared Scout refresh propagates facts but not recruitment state;
- Company/Faction pipeline semantics remain independent;
- Hard/Preferred eligibility rules are deterministic and explainable;
- raw Match remains visible when a profile/vacancy Hard rule makes the player ineligible;
- waivers are per requirement and historically traceable;
- pinned recruiter decisions are never silently overwritten by automated suggestions;
- no automatic Torn message sending;
- no Torn API scheduler bypass;
- no destructive database migration;
- no expansion of Global Intelligence private-data sharing without explicit review;
- shared intelligence pages never become combined Company/Faction recruitment feeds;
- all major recruitment functions remain individually routable rather than collapsed into mega-pages.

## 24. Architectural summary

The platform has three fundamentally different information domains:

1. **Shared truth about the player** -> Player Intelligence.
2. **Our relationship with the player as a potential employee** -> Company Recruitment.
3. **Our relationship with the player as a potential faction member** -> Faction Recruitment.

All future features must respect those boundaries in storage, routing, scoring, history, privacy and tests.
